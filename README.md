## The map

**The geometry is not Figma's.** `assets/map-desktop.svg` and
`assets/map-mobile.svg` are generated by `scripts/gen_map.py` from the sibling
`election-hub-design` prototype: pre-projected albersUsa outlines from us-atlas
(`states-albers-10m`), viewBox 975 × 610. The Figma shapes were hand traced —
coarser borders, and the mobile frame carried a second, faulty set where Montana
came out with a chunk missing. A real projection fixes both, and one geometry now
serves both breakpoints, fitted into the box each card reserves for the map.

**Ratings still come from Figma** (`assets/ratings.json`), so the map reads
exactly as the frame does; only the outlines changed. Each state is a
`<g class="state r-*">` holding the shape (`.st`) and its abbreviation (`.lbl`),
and fills come from the group's class — recolouring never touches geometry.

Abbreviations are `<text>` placed at each state's *pole of inaccessibility*, the
centre of the largest circle that fits inside the shape. A centroid will not do:
on concave states (FL, LA, MI, ID) it lands outside the painted area. Size scales
with that inscribed radius, and states too small to hold two letters are left
unlabelled — DC, DE and RI on desktop, thirteen on the smaller mobile map.

Label colour is black or white, whichever contrasts more against the fill, worst
case **5.0:1** (`Likely D`). Figma cuts the abbreviation out of the shape as a
hole, which leaves the colour hostage to the background and makes AL and AR over
`Safe R` nearly invisible.

## Theme

Follows the system by default. The control sits at the end of the feed filter
row — same round sun/moon button as the `election-hub-design` prototype — and
persists to `localStorage`. `data-theme="light"` / `"dark"` on `<html>` beats the media
query in both directions.

## Where the Figma frames disagree with each other

Three places where the mobile and desktop frames contradict each other. Desktop
wins in all three, so the product never shows two different numbers for the same
thing:

1. **Seat bar** — desktop has three segments (639 / 18 / 654 of 1312, i.e.
   48.7% D · 1.4% toss · 49.9% R); mobile has only two (146 / 215, no toss-up
   band). Desktop's proportions are used everywhere, 8px tall on desktop and 4px
   on mobile as drawn.
2. **New Hampshire** — `Toss-up` on the desktop map, grey on mobile. It is
   `Toss-up` on both.
3. **Hero headline** — desktop renders "Midterms Electionsand Predictions" on one
   line (the Figma text is missing the space before its line separator); mobile
   breaks in two. Both behaviours are reproduced exactly.

Copied as-is even though they look like slips: HI and DC stay `#151515` in the
light theme too, and five states use a fixed `#8f8f8f` grey that never appears in
the legend.

Not reproduced: the DDHQ/Kalshi/Polymarket radio group exists in the frame but is
positioned outside the card and clipped — it does not show up in Figma's own
render, so it does not show up here either. The mobile `3Dots_Drawer` overlays
are likewise outside the frame's flow.

## Map interaction

Figma sources: `Map 2 - Zoom`, `Map 2 - Selected` (desktop) and the `Drawer`
frame (`7845:94309`), which carries the full panel — Texas is the only state with
real content in the file.

The camera is a `<g class="cam">` inside the SVG driven by
`transform: translate() scale()`. Because the SVG covers the whole card
(`viewBox="0 0 1312 811"` with the map offset by `translate(86,42)`), zoom bleeds
to the edges the way the zoom frame shows.

- **wheel / trackpad** → cursor-anchored zoom. The delta is normalised by
  `deltaMode` (mouse wheels report lines, trackpads report pixels) and capped at
  ±120 per event — without the cap one hard flick jumps from 1× to the ceiling.
  Trackpad pinch arrives as wheel + `ctrlKey` and gets a larger step.
- **drag** → pan (only when zoomed; a drag never triggers a selection)
- **two-finger pinch** → zoom on touch
- **double click** → zoom in at the pointer
- **+ / −** → 1.45× steps; **↺** → back to the initial framing
- bounds: 1× to 6×, and the map never leaves the card frame
- on mobile, at rest a finger still scrolls the page over the map
  (`touch-action: pan-y`); once zoomed the gesture belongs to the map

Button tokens, straight from the file: `background/neutral/subtle` on desktop and
`background/neutral/medium` on mobile (the only difference between the two
frames), `border/neutral/bold` for the border, `icon/neutral/bold` when the
button does something and `icon/neutral/bold/disabled` when it does not — which
is why `−` and `↺` look dimmed in the resting frame.

### Selection

**On desktop, selecting reframes the map** — the whole map, not a cut-out of the
state. From whatever zoom the reader was at, the camera goes to 155% and drags
the whole thing until the chosen state sits centred in the free strip left of the
panel. Always the same reading zoom, whether you came from 100% or 400%.

States on the right edge (Maine) can only be framed if the map is allowed past
the frame a little: while a state is selected the pan bound gains slack equal to
the panel width plus half a card. With nothing selected the bound returns to
normal and the map never leaves the card.

On mobile and tablet the camera stays put — the sheet covers nearly everything,
so moving the map underneath it would help nobody.

The selected state gets a `border/inverse/bold` outline (width compensated for
scale, so it reads 2px on screen at any zoom), the others drop to 32% opacity,
and it is moved to the end of the group because SVG has no `z-index`. No shadow:
the outline plus the recede already do the job.

**Selecting works at any zoom level.** This was broken for a decidedly non-obvious
reason: dragging used `setPointerCapture` on the card, and pointer capture makes
the following `click` land on the capturing element — so with zoom active the
click target was never the state's `<g>`. Dragging now listens for
`pointermove`/`pointerup` on the window, with no capture.

The **‹ › arrows** step to the previous/next state alphabetically, reframing the
same way. Keyboard: `←` `→` navigate, `Esc` closes.

### Panel

At ≥1024px it is the modal anchored to the right inside the card (512 wide, 32
from the top and right, as in `Map 2 - Selected`). Below that — tablet and mobile
— it becomes a bottom sheet, 88vh tall and with no grab handle: **it closes on ✕
only**. Clicking the scrim does not close it, and the page body is frozen
(`position: fixed` + `touch-action: none`, with scroll position restored on
close), so there is no way to scroll the page behind the modal.

Top bar (80) and bottom bar (65) stay fixed; only the middle scrolls — the same
split as the `Drawer` frame, where the scrollable block of 949 holds the state
header, the source tabs, Candidates and Districts.

**Every panel is the same height**, race or no race — fixed at `100% - 64px` of
the card on desktop and 88vh in the sheet — so the modal never jumps size between
clicks.

Panel shadow is Figma's (250 blur, black at 100%) in dark; in light it drops to
**8%**, because at 100% it turns into a grey smear around the card.

With the sheet open the map takes **no zoom at all** — wheel, pinch, drag and all
three buttons stay inert until it closes. A wheel over the panel scrolls the
panel and nothing else (`overscroll-behavior: contain`, plus the zoom handler
ignores events originating inside `.panel`).

### Race and Source

The two map pills are real selects:

- **Race** — House · Senate · Governor
- **Source** — DDHQ · Kalshi · Polymarket

The source tabs inside the modal and the Source pill are **two views of the same
state**: changing one changes the other, and the whole map repaints immediately.
On mobile both pills are centred across the card.

The **seat bar** follows. The frame's numbers (470 D · 60 toss · 1,000 R) and its
widths (639/18/654 of 1312) hold for House + Polymarket, and switching race or
source moves all three in proportion to how far the map's rating distribution has
drifted from that baseline. Worth noting: in the file the bar and the numbers
beside it do not agree with each other — the bar is the current seat composition,
the numbers are the projection — so each moves on its own scale.

The map Figma drew is **House with the pill on Polymarket**; that is the initial
state and it comes out identical to the frame. DDHQ and Kalshi shift each state
up to two steps along the rating ramp (the premise being that a prediction market
and a polling average read the race differently); Senate and Governor redraw both
who is on the ballot and how each is rated. All deterministic from the state
code: same combination, same map, every time.

Race numbers (`js/data.js`) follow the same logic — Figma only has Texas's
content. Districts only appear for the House; Senate and Governor are one office
per state. Flags came from `election-hub-design/public/flags`.

## Countdown

The frame's "Elections will start in 3d 5h 20m" is the starting point: on load
the page pins the target 3d 5h 20m out, and from there the clock actually runs,
ticking every second. Seconds are always visible (`3d 5h 19m 54s`) so you can
watch time move; days and hours drop off on their own once they hit zero. The
number uses `tabular-nums` and a minimum width so the line does not jitter on
each tick.

## States with no race

Grey states on the map (`off`, `blank` and `nodata`) do open the panel — into an
**empty state**: under the state name it reads "No 2026 election in Alaska yet.",
with a line explaining nothing is on that chamber's ballot. Source tabs,
candidates and districts are not rendered. The message sits at the top, exactly
where the subtitle would be; the space left below simply stays empty. The ‹ ›
arrows are still there, so you can leave an empty state without closing. Changing
Race can take a state off the ballot or put it back, and the panel follows.

## Feed filters

The chips under "All Election Coverage" actually filter: each card carries a
`data-kind` (episode, article, short, opinion) and the chips match against it.
Cards leaving fade and collapse; the ones staying rise back in on a 55ms stagger,
so the list reflows instead of snapping. A day heading left with nothing under it
collapses with its cards.

## Motion

Four durations and one curve, in `tokens.css` (`--dur-micro/short/medium/long`,
`--ease`), so the whole thing reads as one system rather than a pile of separate
animations:

- **candidate bars** grow left to right when the panel opens, cascading 70ms
  between rows (width goes 0 → target in 620ms);
- **panel content** enters on a short offset, staggered per section;
- **the map camera** travels in 620ms;
- **recolouring the map** (race/source change) is a `fill` transition, not a cut —
  same for the seat bar segments, which slide;
- **pill menus** open and close on opacity and scale; `hidden` is applied only
  after the transition, so they do not vanish before finishing;
- **theme switching** transitions the surfaces instead of flashing;
- everything is off under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`.
